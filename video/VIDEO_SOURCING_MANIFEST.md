# History Hack — Downloadable Public-Domain Video Sourcing Manifest
**Scope:** Grade 6 · Grade 7 · Grade 8 · Tennessee · World · U.S. · Government (7 courses)
**Rule:** Only video that is (a) public domain or explicitly free-to-reuse, (b) **cleared for commercial use**, (c) **downloadable** so you host it on your own server. No YouTube, no third-party players.
**Status of this document:** Source directory + per-course map + verified download entry points + a per-clip schema. Individual clip URLs should be verified against the checklist (§4) before hosting — see "How to read this" below.

> This is AI-assisted sourcing, not legal advice. For a commercial product, have counsel confirm the clearance basis you rely on.

### Discovery tools — find here, but NEVER cite (fails Schedule F provenance)
| Tool | Role | Why not a source |
|---|---|---|
| **Wikimedia Commons** · **Internet Archive (bare uploads)** · **DPLA** | Locate candidate footage only | User-uploaded / aggregated; provenance is user-asserted and titles are often editorialized. **Trace every hit to its originating institution (NARA, LoC, etc.), verify + download there, and cite THAT.** An aggregator is never the source of record. (Wikimedia WebM → transcode to MP4/HLS.) |

**Academic-defensibility tiers:** Rows 1–3 + TeVA (institution) = **Tier 1**, cite directly. Rows 4–5 (Prelinger, Universal Newsreels) = **Tier 2**, cite the collection/origin, verify per item. Discovery tools above = **Tier 3**, never cited.

**Avoid:** CriticalPast and similar "PD stock footage" resellers — they charge licensing fees for footage that is often PD at the true source. Go to the source, not the reseller.

## 4. Per-clip clearance checklist (run before you host anything)

A clip is **ACCEPTED** only if ALL are true:
1. [ ] **Rights (Axis 1)** — basis is explicit and verifiable on the item page (federal work §105 / pre-1929 / stated PD or CC dedication) and permits **commercial** use.
2. [ ] **Provenance (Axis 2)** — traces to a **named authoritative institution** (Tier 1/2). The citation names that institution, **not** an aggregator. *(Found on Wikimedia/Internet Archive/DPLA? Trace to the origin and cite that, or reject.)*
3. [ ] **Institutional URL / accession** recorded (stable, re-findable by a reviewer).
4. [ ] Downloadable and legal to self-host (you have the file, not a stream you're hotlinking).
5. [ ] **Not** a special-session/licensed carve-out (C-SPAN SOTU/joint session; C-SPAN-produced programming).
6. [ ] **If Tennessee/state/local:** item is tagged PD / "No Known Copyright," not merely "educational fair use."
7. [ ] Attribution recorded as the institution states it.
8. [ ] Correct course + standard.
When unsure → **reject** and leave the slot empty. Default is "no video," never "unverified video." An aggregator-only origin fails check 2 — it is not adoption-eligible.

## 6. Honest limitations
- **This manifest gives you every vetted, commercial-safe, downloadable source and the real entry points for all 7 courses.** Building the exhaustive *per-standard clip list* (one verified download URL per standard × 7 courses) is a large, ongoing pass — best done course-by-course so each URL is individually checked, not bulk-guessed.
- **Ancient/medieval (Grade 6–7) and Tennessee** will have sparse video by nature; plan those around your primary-source image bank.
- **Transcode note:** Wikimedia delivers WebM; you'll transcode to MP4/HLS for your player (one-time `ffmpeg`).
